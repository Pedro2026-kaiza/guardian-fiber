The brief

What the role actually asks

Innovation Lead, Digital Center of Excellence — individual contributor, matrixed teams.

Lead exploration across four domains

Identify and explore new business and technology opportunities across Customer, Employee, Factory, and Product experience domains, with cross-functional teams.

Run workshops

Facilitate exploration and "big idea" sessions with designers, architects, and business stakeholders to brainstorm and develop ideas.

Execute proofs of concept

Lead POC execution using the DICE agile framework and Design Thinking methodologies, with internal and external partners.

Bridge vision and execution

Translate technical concepts into actionable strategy — communicating clearly to every level of the organization, including executives.

At first read, this looks like a mandate to build an innovation function from nothing.
Starting point

What already exists

Public

DICE: three existing programs

Education builds digital fluency. Lab provides the POC methodology across the same four domains named in this posting. Outreach connects internal and external experts, universities, and suppliers.

A disciplined pace, not a blank process

One week maximum to validate a hypothesis. One month maximum for a proof of concept — a limit the CIO has publicly admitted the company once got wrong, letting POCs run for months.

Decentralized by design

Each business unit builds its own product and software capability. IT provides shared infrastructure, security, and a common architecture (iCore).

A philosophy already in place

"We don't want to build a lot of AI" — Nick Colisto, CIO. Commercial platforms over homegrown tools, humans in the loop, culture before tooling.

Reframe

The real opportunity isn't architecture

Five years of structure already exist. What's scarce is judgment about which bet to run next.

"That judgment is best shown, not claimed."

The rest of this case works through one real, dated, public pressure point the company already faces — all the way to a proof-of-concept plan written in the company's own process language.
The trigger

The real trigger: the Digital Product Passport

Public

A real regulation, a real date

The EU's Digital Product Passport requirement begins in 2027, starting with two categories: batteries, and apparel and textiles.

Already on the roadmap

atma.io, Avery Dennison's own connected-product platform, already markets a "DPP as a Service" offering. This is public positioning, not speculation.

Why it matters now

Apparel and textiles are already core Avery Dennison categories. This isn't a hypothetical new market — it's the one they already serve.

Three horizons

Where this could go, in order of certainty

Each horizon guards something different — the brand's compliance, the product's authenticity, and the wearer's body — built on the same fiber.

01
Compliance · the near-certain bet

Horizon 1

What it is

Use the RFID and label infrastructure Avery Dennison already sells, feeding atma.io, to get apparel and textile customers audit-ready before the 2027 deadline.

Why it goes first

Lowest uncertainty of the three horizons: real regulation, real deadline, and an existing product already positioned for exactly this.

This horizon doesn't need invention — it needs execution before the clock runs out.
02
Identity in the fiber · the proven bet

Horizon 2

Proven once already

Avery Dennison acquired TexTrace in 2022 specifically to integrate RFID at the yarn level — inside manufacturing, not added as a label afterward.

What it buys

Higher-fidelity data for the same Digital Product Passport — and a manufacturing capability the company has already paid for and proven, not one to build from zero.

Horizon 2 doesn't ask "can we do this" — Avery Dennison already did, in 2022.
03
Functional material · the exploratory bet

Horizon 3

Exploratory bet — lab-stage research

Thermoregulation

Published 2026 research shows a passive dual-mode fabric that cools on one side (95% solar reflectivity) and heats on the other (up to 18.5°C rise) — no battery, no electronics.

Self-healing

Published 2025 research shows ionic-gel polymer fiber that re-bonds under heat or pressure, recovering about 70% of original strength within an hour — already moving toward industrial partnership.

Avery Dennison already tried this ambition once.

Between 2016 and 2019, its Janela platform aimed to connect 10 billion garments — identity only, nothing physical. The science to make the garment itself intelligent didn't exist then. Parts of it exist now.
Execution model

Speaking the process they already have

Applying DICE's own stage-gate to Horizon 1.

Week 1 — hypothesis

Validate, with two or three real apparel customers already using Avery Dennison RFID, whether DPP compliance is an active 2026–2027 budget conversation.

Weeks 2–5 — proof of concept

A working DPP record for one SKU, end to end: label → atma.io record → QR-accessible passport — inside the one-month limit DICE already holds itself to.

What this demonstrates

Not a new framework proposed from outside — fluency inside the one that already exists.

Scope honesty

What's real, and what's a bet

What's real

DICE's structure, its three programs, the four domains, the POC discipline, atma.io, TexTrace, and the 2027 DPP deadline — all public and independently verifiable.

What's a bet

That Horizon 3's materials are the right next bet for this specific company, and that this exact sequencing is how DICE would choose to prioritize it — that's judgment, not fact.

This case doesn't arrive at a closed answer — it arrives at someone capable of reaching one with real information.
